### Definition Definition

Market Structure in ICTs methodology is the **first and most critical layer** in building a trading narrative. It represents the *real-time footprint* of Smart Money  showing whether they are **accumulating (bullish)** or **distributing (bearish)**.

A **bullish structure** is defined by price making **Higher Highs (HH)** and **Higher Lows (HL)**. A **bearish structure** is formed when price creates **Lower Lows (LL)** and **Lower Highs (LH)**. These sequences visually represent order flow and institutional intent.

ICT teaches that structure is only valid when confirmed by **displacement**  a strong, impulsive price movement that decisively breaks a previous swing point.
Without displacement, a break is not structure  its a **liquidity raid**, and the move is likely to reverse.

Market Structure is the **entry point** to every ICT model: you do not define liquidity draws, anticipate setups, or validate SMT without first establishing trend direction through structure.

Brain Term: Break of Structure (BOS) vs Shift in Market Structure (SMS)

This is Entry #2  Break of Structure vs Shift in Market Structure (BOS vs SMS)  a critical distinction that separates continuation from reversal, and one of the most misunderstood concepts in ICTs teachings.

Definition Definition

A Break of Structure (BOS) occurs when price breaks a previous swing point in the same direction as the prevailing trend, with visible displacement. It confirms trend continuation. For example, in a bullish trend, a BOS happens when price forms a Higher High (HH) that breaks the previous HH with strength.

A Shift in Market Structure (SMS), by contrast, is when price breaks a swing point against the current trend, signaling a potential reversal. An SMS is only valid if the break occurs with clear displacement, not just a wick or soft break. ICT is explicit that a true SMS requires the destruction of a prior swing low or high, depending on the direction of the shift, and must suggest a change in institutional intent.

While a BOS confirms Smart Money is continuing in the same direction, an SMS is your first clue that they are done accumulating or distributing  and now moving price the other way.

Image What This Image Would Show (ICT-Authentic)

The chart would show two separate zones: one where BOS is confirmed, another where SMS causes trend reversal.

Left Side  BOS Example

Price forms HL  HH  HL  new HH.

That HH breaks the previous high with a strong bullish candle, showing clear BOS.

This confirms the trend is continuing in the same direction.

Right Side  SMS Example

Price makes HH  HL, but then instead of a new HH, it drops sharply.

A displacement candle breaks below the last HL.

This move is labeled as SMS. After that, price forms LL  LH, beginning a new bearish structure.

No indicators are needed. The strength of the break and the direction of displacement tell you whether its BOS (continuation) or SMS (trend shift).
ICT emphasizes using this concept on 1H and 15M, especially when preparing for intraday setups or framing weekly bias.

Application Application Notes

[X] Use BOS to confirm existing trend bias  enter on retracements (like OTE or FVG) after a BOS.
[X] An SMS is not an entry signal by itself. It tells you bias has changed, and you must wait for structure to form in the new direction before entering.
[X] BOS without displacement is noise. SMS without displacement is a liquidity trap  wait for conviction in both cases.
[X] ICT often uses SMS to anticipate Judas Swings or Silver Bullet setups when bias flips during a Killzone.

This concept is the backbone of ICTs execution models. FVGs show where Smart Money left the market unbalanced, and where theyre likely to return to reprice efficiently.

Definition Definition

A Fair Value Gap (FVG) is a specific three-candle price formation that highlights a moment of imbalance, where institutional buying or selling occurred so aggressively that the market didnt offer the opposite side a fair chance to transact.

Its formed when the high of candle 1 is below the low of candle 3 in a bullish scenario  meaning candle 2 exploded through that area. The gap between candle 1's high and candle 3's low is the Fair Value Gap zone.

In ICTs models, FVGs are critical because they:

Confirm displacement (institutional order flow)

Act as entry points during retracements

Represent zones Smart Money left behind, and may return to mitigate or rebalance price

A valid FVG should always follow a BOS or SMS  its a confirmation that the move was powered by institutions, not retail noise.

On a candlestick chart (typically 15M or 5M for execution):

Bullish FVG Example:

Candle 1 closes bullish.

Candle 2 is a large bullish displacement candle.

Candle 3 opens higher but doesnt fill the gap left by candle 2.

The gap between candle 1s high and candle 3s low is shaded and labeled as the FVG.

Bearish FVG Example:

Candle 1 closes bearish.

Candle 2 is a large bearish displacement candle.

Candle 3 opens lower, leaving a visible imbalance.

The space between candle 1s low and candle 3s high is the FVG.

ICT often draws a box over the FVG and waits for price to retrace into it, tap it, then continue in the direction of the original move.

[X] Not all gaps are valid  watch for displacement first, then find the FVG in the immediate retracement.

Definition Definition

The key level inside the OTE zone is 70.5%, which ICT calls the "Smart Money entry point". This model is only applied after a confirmed displacement and BOS/SMS, and is most effective during Killzones when aligned with liquidity targets.

A Bearish OB is the last up candle before a bearish displacement.

Order Blocks are not trade signals on their own  they require confirmation via structure, displacement, and time. However, when price revisits them during a retracement, they offer precision entry in line with institutional logic.
